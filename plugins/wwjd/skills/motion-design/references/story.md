# Story: what the piece says, shot by shot

## Structure
- **One message, ≤ 15 words** (S1): simplify, focus, tell it economically (StoryBrand; Pixar #5, #22).
- **And → But → Therefore** (Randy Olson): context, problem, solution. Replace every "and then" with "but" or "therefore". Longer pieces: the story spine, every day… but one day… because of that (× 3)… until finally… ever since (Kenn Adams).
- **The contract** (Lago): "because the person does X, the product changes Y, giving Z". Delete a shot that serves none of X, Y, Z.
- **The viewer's world is the hero, the product the guide** (StoryBrand): the persona's own material (an inbox, a sheet, a file) carries the story; the product acts on it.
- **A scene changes something** (McKee): name each shot's before and after.
- **Context before detail** (Common Craft): a few seconds of establishing shot, then go close.
- **The problem reads from a still:** a pile, a flood, red counts, a mess that says "too much" with no caption. Resolve it with the same objects, not new ones.
- **When the product works on its own, show the absence of a hand:** after the one setup action the pointer leaves and the rest happens untouched. That absence is the payoff.

## Hook
- Subject on screen by 1 s, a hero's message readable by 3 s (Google ABCD: product in the first 5 s, tight framing). Visitors decide in about 10 s (NN/g).
- A hero headline is page text (an h1) from first paint, never revealed by the animation.

## Shots
- **3-6 s each;** explainers run 8-20 shots a minute. Live-action ads cut every 1-2 s; UI can't be read at that pace.
- **Every transition is a sentence** (Material, NN/g):
  | Relation | Transition |
  |---|---|
  | the same thing, changed | morph or container transform; a match cut on shape |
  | the next step | one axis, one direction (forward keeps its direction; reverse means back) |
  | going deeper / back out | push in / pull out |
  | something travels somewhere | the camera follows it, as far as it has gone |
  | unrelated | fade through |
- Plan transitions with the story, scene by scene, not after (Buck).
- **One space with logic:** a source sits where it comes from (e.g. the upload area above the list it fills), so every move's direction means something. Nothing swaps in place.
- **A showy move is followed by a quiet one** (ST5; Vercel homepage).

## Endings
- **Decide the ending first** (Pixar #7) and write it as a still.
- **Rest on the payoff:** everything still ≥ 2 s; never stop mid-motion.
- **Loops:** the last frame sits one step before the first and the seam falls at the slowest moment; or fade out in place and fade frame 0 in. The camera never travels home. Banner-ad practice: ≤ 15 s of animation, ≤ 3 loops, then rest.

## Scroll-told stories
- Readers want to scroll (NYT graphics desk): scroll triggers motion and is never taken over (NN/g scrolljacking, The Pudding).
- The graphic is pinned while text steps pass; each step's change finishes in its first ~40%, the rest is a reading hold.
- Only where the transition carries meaning; keyboard and history keep working.
