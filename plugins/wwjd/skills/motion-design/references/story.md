# Story: what the piece says, shot by shot

## Structure
- **One message, ≤ 15 words.** Every shot serves it. "If you confuse, you'll lose" (StoryBrand). Simplify, focus, tell it economically (Pixar #5, #22).
- **And → But → Therefore** (Randy Olson): context, problem, solution. Replace every "and then" with "but" or "therefore"; a chain of "and"s is a list. For longer pieces the story spine: every day… but one day… because of that (× 3)… until finally… ever since (Kenn Adams).
- **The contract** (Lago): "because the person does X, the product changes Y, giving Z". A shot that serves none of X, Y, Z is deleted.
- **The viewer's world is the hero, the product is the guide** (StoryBrand): the persona's inbox, sheet or file carries the story; the product acts on it.
- **A scene must change something** (McKee): name each shot's before and after. Nothing changed, cut it.
- **Context before detail:** forest, tree, leaves (Common Craft). An establishing shot lasts a few seconds, then go close.
- **Show the problem as a state you can see:** a pile, a flood, red counts, a mess that reads as "too much" from a still, with no caption. Resolve it with the same objects, not new ones. A promise is not a demonstration.
- **Problem → one action → what it sets off → result.** "Automatic" means no hand: after the one setup the hand leaves, and the next thing is handled with nobody touching it. That absence is the payoff.

## Hook
- The subject is on screen by 1 s; a hero's message readable by 3 s (Google ABCD: brand or product in the first 5 s, tight framing, more than two frames in those 5 s). Visitors decide in about 10 s (NN/g).
- A hero headline is text in the page from the first paint (an h1), never something the animation reveals.

## Shots
- **One subject, one action, one change** per shot; 3-6 s each. Live-action ads cut every 1-2 s; product and UI motion can't be read at that pace. Explainers run 8-20 shots a minute.
- **One subject in frame at rest:** neighbours more than half a frame away, nothing peeking at the edge.
- **Every transition is a sentence** (Material, NN/g):
  | Relation | Transition |
  |---|---|
  | the same thing, changed | morph or container transform; a match cut on shape |
  | the next step | one axis, one direction (forward keeps its direction; reverse means back) |
  | going deeper / back out | push in / pull out |
  | the data goes somewhere | the camera follows it, as far as it has gone |
  | unrelated | fade through |
- Plan transitions with the story, not after (Buck went through the script scene by scene for "imagery for each scene and the transitions between them").
- **One space with logic:** sources sit where they come from (the supplier above the rows it fills), so every move has a direction that means something. Nothing swaps in place.
- **At most 2 big moments, never back to back;** a showy move is followed by a quiet one (Vercel homepage).

## Endings
- **Decide the ending first** (Pixar #7) and write it as a still.
- **Rest on the payoff:** everything still for ≥ 2 s; a hero plays once and rests on a settled frame with Replay; never stop mid-motion.
- **Loops:** the last frame sits one step before the first; the seam falls at the slowest moment; or fade out in place and fade frame 0 in. The camera never travels home. Banner-ad practice: ≤ 15 s of animation, ≤ 3 loops, then rest.
- **Reduced motion rests on the final frame,** which doubles as the poster.

## Scroll-told stories
- Readers just want to scroll (NYT graphics desk): motion is triggered by scroll, scroll is never taken over (NN/g scrolljacking, The Pudding).
- The graphic is pinned while text steps pass; **one change per step;** the change finishes in the first ~40% of the step and the rest is a reading hold; scrolling up reverses each step.
- Short and sweet, only where the transition carries meaning; at most 2 pinned sections a page; keyboard and history keep working.

## Mistakes to cut on sight
Too many ideas · explaining instead of showing · captions on every shot · no payoff or a payoff with no hold · ending mid-motion · a "mess" that doesn't look messy · new objects for the solution · the hand doing the automatic part · copying TV-ad pace.
