# Hero patterns

**D** = desktop 1440, **P** = phone 390 (composed on its own), **Act** = where the one action lives, **Ex** = public examples (sites change; check one live before citing it). No Motion line = no motion; any hero motion becomes a still under reduced motion (Floor).

**Phone rule first:** consent sheets can cover **50–60% of a 390 viewport**, so the phone action sits in the **top ~45%**.

## 1. Tool-as-hero: the core input works on screen one
- Fits: delivery, rides, travel, domains, file tools: the first step is an input. Avoid: no single first input.
- D: short claim over the input, real results under it. P: input right under the nav, claim 3–5 words.
- Act: submit **inside** the widget ("See prices", "Upload image"); no separate CTA.
- Ex: uber.com
- Tells: the input is a fake link to signup; no sample data to try.

## 2. Local answer: "where are you?" returns the decisive fact
- Fits: place or time changes the answer (delivery, stock, availability, hours). Avoid: a guess the visitor cannot correct.
- D: detected place as editable text ("Stockholm · change"), answer beside it. P: place + answer on one line.
- Act: the answer is the button ("Safeway · 25 min" opens the store).
- Motion: swap the answer instantly on change.
- Ex: instacart.com
- Tells: location detected but not shown; a country modal first.

## 3. Headline-as-query: the H1 is a search the visitor can run
- Fits: discovery products. Avoid: single-purpose tools.
- D: centred H1 ending in a rotating noun plus an inline object image, field below. P: same; CTA becomes "Download app".
- Act: the field.
- Motion: one, the noun swap.
- Ex: cosmos.so ("Find your next lamp with character")
- Tells: rotating words that are not real results; rotation faster than reading speed.

## 4. Live product: the real UI running, not a mockup
- Fits: SaaS, devtools, editors. Avoid: a generic or empty UI.
- D: claim top-left, full-width app window bleeding off the bottom. P: a cropped corner at real scale, never the whole window shrunk.
- Act: Download/Sign up beside the claim; the demo is proof, not the action.
- Motion: one loop of real work.
- Ex: linear.app
- Tells: lorem data, fake cursor, endless motion, an unreadable 40%-scale window.

## 5. Prompt box: the product is a conversation
- Fits: AI builders, search, assistants.
- D: one question as H1 ("What will you build today?"), wide box, 3–5 starter chips. P: box full width, chips scroll sideways.
- Act: Enter/send **in the box**; signup after the first prompt.
- Motion: placeholder types one example, then stops.
- Ex: lovable.dev
- Tells: generic chips; typing hits a signup wall.

## 6. Type-only statement
- Fits: a claim strong enough to stand alone. Avoid: generic claims ("Build faster").
- D: display H1 ≤2 lines, one subline, one action. P: H1 in 3–4 lines.
- Act: directly under the H1.
- Ex: wise.com
- Tells: big type around a weak sentence.

## 7. Plain document: reads like a README
- Fits: tools for thinkers, open source, anti-marketing brands. Avoid: mass consumer.
- D: narrow text column, a lettered list, one exact live fact. P: identical.
- Act: one small inline "Sign up".
- Ex: are.na ("For the last 15 years and 68 days", computed daily)
- Tells: affectation without content.

## 8. Full-bleed scene
- Fits: vehicles, film, places, hospitality. Avoid: SaaS stock desk photos.
- D: edge-to-edge photo/video, action in its quiet zone. P: a portrait re-crop, not a centred landscape crop.
- Act: one field or button on the image.
- Motion: one slow loop, pausable.
- Ex: a24films.com
- Tells: text on a busy area.

## 9. Product object
- Fits: hardware, physical goods. Avoid: software.
- D: object large on a quiet ground; name, claim, price + Buy. P: object, then the Buy row.
- Act: price and Buy **next to the object**.
- Motion: one turn or reveal on load.
- Ex: apple.com/airpods-pro
- Tells: floating render, no scale cue, hidden price.

## 10. Toy: the first screen is something to play with
- Fits: taste-led brands, creative tools, music, games. Avoid: B2B buyers on a deadline.
- D: one interactive object, one-line caption. P: tap version, or a still saying "play on desktop".
- Act: playing **is** the action, with a quiet way out to Get/Download.
- Motion: user-driven only.
- Ex: poolsuite.net
- Tells: nothing says what it is.

## 11. Before → after
- Fits: enhancement, cleanup, audio, editing, renovation. Avoid: a difference too subtle at 390.
- D: one large frame, drag divider, before on the left. P: full-width frame with a tap toggle, not a drag.
- Act: "Try with your image" **on the frame**.
- Motion: one auto-sweep on load, then the visitor drives.
- Ex: letsenhance.io (below the fold, like most of the category: a hero version is open territory)
- Tells: cherry-picked miracle, mismatched crops.

## 12. Editorial cover
- Fits: media, publishers, books. Avoid: transactional products.
- D: one story as cover (headline, dek, Read now). P: image top, headline overlapping its bottom edge.
- Act: "Read now" under the dek.
- Motion: optional, one image treatment.
- Ex: every.to
- Tells: a grid of 9 equal stories means no cover.

## 13. Number as the claim
- Fits: only when the number **is** the pitch (odds, rates, scale).
- D: one number at display size, unit + source, claim as caption. P: number first.
- Act: next to the number ("See markets", "Open account").
- Motion: a live tick only if the data is live.
- Ex: stripe.com (live GDP share)
- Tells: round numbers; count-up on a static figure.

## 14. Split with a reason: the product has two sides
- Fits: translators, converters, schedulers, code↔render.
- D: the halves map to the product's own two panes. P: stacked in usage order (input above output).
- Act: inside the working half.
- Motion: optional, the widget's own state change.
- Ex: deepl.com/translator
- Tells: the right half could be any picture and nothing changes.

## 15. Centred minimal
- Fits: a known app whose icon carries it. Avoid: unknown products.
- D: icon, name, one sentence, one action, then air. P: the same.
- Act: the one button.
- Motion: optional, the product rendering itself.
- Ex: culturedcode.com/things
- Tells: centred only because it is the default.

## 16. Scroll-told
- Fits: flagship hardware, one-off launches. Avoid: anything people revisit.
- D: pinned stage, scroll scrubs one sequence. P: shorter, never scroll-jacked.
- Act: Buy stays visible in a sticky bar throughout.
- Motion: scroll-linked; a static stack under reduced motion. Story beats: `wwjd:motion-design`.
- Ex: apple.com/airpods-pro
- Tells: screen one says nothing until scrolled; hijacked scroll speed.

## 17. Wall of real use
- Fits: marketplaces, communities, templates: proof that **is** the inventory.
- D: claim + action over a wall of real items. P: one swipeable row.
- Act: the claim's input, or the items themselves link in.
- Motion: one slow marquee, paused on hover/focus.
- Ex: wolt.com
- Tells: non-customer logos; items that link nowhere.

## 18. One field (waitlist / signup)
- Fits: pre-launch, B2B where email is step one. Avoid: when visitors could try instead.
- D: claim, field + button on one line, a promise under it. P: stacked, top half.
- Act: the field; say what happens next ("One email, the day it's ready").
- Motion: optional, one mascot moment.
- Ex: ramp.com ("What's your work email?")
- Tells: "Join 10,000+ on the waitlist"; a second CTA beside the field.

## 19. Finder: one question at a time to the right product
- Fits: considered purchases (chairs, cars, plans).
- D: question 1 and its answers are the hero. P: one card per question, thumb-reachable answers.
- Act: each answer advances; the result ends in Buy.
- Motion: a card-to-card transition only.
- Ex: findmy.vitra.com
- Tells: >5 questions; an email gate before the result.

## Information grading
Grade every fact (`budgets.md`): on a hero the deciding fact sits next to the action, the delight gets its own beat (never a bullet), the rest is cut. Example, a flight tracker: "Landed 6:32am (22m early)" decides; "☀️ 88º" delights. Test: if a competitor could say it unchanged, it is nothing.

## Bans
- Left text / right image as the default; use 14 only with a reason.
- Eyebrow kicker over the H1; a striking number gets its own line (13), not a kicker.
- A gradient word in the headline.
- Fake or uncleared logo strips; round-number stat strips ("10k+ · 99.9% · 4.9★").
- Fade-up on everything: at most one interruptible moment.
- **A CTA split from the widget that motivates it:** the action lives inside the experience ("See prices" in the widget, not "Get started" in the nav).
- Carousel heroes, autoplay sound, blank without WebGL/JS.
- A phone hero that is the desktop stacked, or an action below the consent-sheet line.
