# distill: the removal pass

Use for "too busy", "simplify", "clean up", or "I got overloaded". A good designer removes rather than adds. Sweep the whole scope and fix each pattern at its source.

## Delete
- **Explaining text:**
  - Intro lines and "how this works" copy.
  - Helper text that restates the label.
  - Legends that repeat the axis.
  - Headers above things that already say what they are.
  - Implementation commentary inside UI or mock data.
- **Reassurance and narration:** "Nothing is ever lost", "Everything is private", "When you click X we will…".
- **Duplicates:** a second control for the same job, the same status said twice ("Draft" plus "Unpublished"), two paths to the same place, the same avatar twice in a view.
- **Unearned numbers:** counts that change no decision, "N of M", zero counts. Turn them into a dot, a progress mark, or nothing.
- **Unneeded controls:** toggles, filters, sort and search that the task doesn't need. Short lists need none.
- **Machinery receipts:** steps run, files read, "AI is thinking…" once the result is visible.

## Reduce
- **Status:** boil many statuses down to the one or two that need a person; everything else stays calm.
- **Choices:** default instead of asking. Remove steps, confirmations and choices that have an obvious answer.
- **Disclosure:** move rare actions and detail one layer down (`prioritize.md`).
- **Structure:** remove a card, border or section that only exists to hold one thing.
- **Labels:** one internal concept per label. A label or tier that fuses two backend concepts ("Embedded · Cashback") is split or flattened.

## Not this
- **Keep legal, consent, pricing and safety text.**
- **Don't strip full content from Persuade and Editorial.** Thin is not the same as clean. Removal means fewer elements with stronger hierarchy, not less design.
- **Don't fill freed space with ornament.** Close the gap, or let what remains grow.
- **Check who loses a control before removing it:** power users, keyboard and assistive-tech users, automation (selectors, deep links, extensions). If the habit wouldn't come back when the control is re-added later, the removal is one-way.

**Done when** nothing left can go without breaking the task.
